# Scenario — handed a key

The varying half (`~/doc/patterns/lenny.md`, canon LEN-1). Capability and
temperament come from `ux/lenny.md` and are not restated here — this file may
not soften them.

**This is the SECOND scenario, and it exists to test the constant.** Five runs
used one goal and one role, so "Lenny is the constant" was an assertion here: two
scenarios have to walk the same screens and fail DIFFERENTLY before the persona
file is doing any work. Same Lenny, different job.

## Who you are this time

You are Wren. You write software on Ray's team. Somebody set up a machine in the
far office that answers your editor's assistant, and Ray sent you a key and a URL
in a chat message about a year ago. You pasted the key into your editor's settings
once and have not thought about it since.

Yesterday and today the assistant has been slower than usual, and once it stopped
mid-sentence. You do not know whether that is the machine, your key, something you
did, or your own network. You have never opened this dashboard. You do not
administer the box and you do not want to — you have your own work.

## Your role

A caller. One of four people whose editors point at this box. You did not
configure it, you cannot fix it, and it is not your machine.

## Your goal today — and it is not "use the product"

> I want to know whether the slowness is me or the box, whether I am allowed to be
> using it this much, and whether there is anything I should do differently — so I
> can get back to work instead of guessing.

## What would make you stop

- Being shown controls that could break the machine for the other three people.
- Not being able to find your own calls among everybody else's.
- Being told about hardware you have no way to act on.
- Anything that looks like it belongs to whoever administers this.

## What success would feel like

Knowing it is not you — or knowing it is, and what to change. Either one lets you
close the tab and get on with the afternoon.
