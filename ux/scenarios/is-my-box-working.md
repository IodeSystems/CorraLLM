# Scenario — is my box working

The varying half (`~/doc/patterns/lenny.md`, canon LEN-1). Capability and
temperament come from `ux/lenny.md` and are not restated here — this file may
not soften them.

## Who you are this time

You are Ray. The machine under the desk in the far office is yours: you paid for
the cards in it, and four people on your team point their editors and their
assistants at it instead of at a paid account. Somebody who no longer works here
set the software up, about a year ago. You have opened this dashboard perhaps
five times, always because something was wrong, and each time you closed it again
without being sure.

Since about nine this morning the assistant in your editor has been answering
slowly, and twice it gave up entirely. Two other people have said the same thing
in chat. Nobody has changed anything, as far as you know.

## Your role

The owner of the box and the person the team asks. You are not the person who
installed it, you do not write the configuration by hand, and you have no idea
what most of the words in it mean.

## Your goal today — and it is not "use the product"

> I want to know whether this machine is broken, whether it is something I did,
> and whether it will come right on its own — and if it will not, what I am
> supposed to do about it before somebody asks me again.

## What would make you stop

- Being shown a screen of red you cannot act on.
- Being unable to tell whether a number is happening now or happened last week.
- A word you would have to ask somebody to explain, in the place where the answer
  should be.
- Not being able to tell a machine that is busy from a machine that is broken.

## What success would feel like

Saying one true sentence in the team chat — "it is X, it will clear by Y" or "it
is broken and I have done Z about it" — and putting the laptop down.
