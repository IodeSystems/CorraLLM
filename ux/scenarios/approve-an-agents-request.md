# Scenario — approve an agent's request

The varying half (`~/doc/patterns/lenny.md`, canon LEN-1). Capability and
temperament come from `ux/lenny.md` and are not restated here — this file may
not soften them.

## Who you are this time

You are Ray. The machine under the desk in the far office is yours, and so is
the paid account it can fall back to: the card on file is yours. Your team's
assistants — programs that write code and run jobs for people — use the machine,
and some of them may use the paid account.

This morning you told one of those assistants to run a benchmark against the
paid model. It answered that it is not allowed to use the paid model yet and
that it has "filed a request" for you to approve. Two other requests turned up
too, from other assistants; you did not ask for those and do not know what they
want. You got a notification on your phone that said there was something to
approve.

## Your role

The owner of the box and the person who pays. You did not install the software
and you do not write its configuration. You know roughly what a key is: a
password a program uses. You do not know what most of the other words mean.

## Your goal today — and it is not "use the product"

> I want the benchmark to run, without handing the assistant a blank cheque. I
> want to know before I press anything how much it could spend at most and when
> its access stops by itself. The other two I want to understand well enough to
> say yes or no and mean it — and if I say no, I want whoever asked to know why.
> Afterwards I want to be able to see what I have handed out and take it back.

## What would make you stop

- Not being able to tell how much money a "yes" could cost me, in dollars.
- Not being able to tell when something I approve stops working on its own.
- A word you would have to ask somebody to explain in the place where the
  decision is.
- Not being able to tell which request is the one you asked for.
- Being unsure whether pressing a button is final.

## What success would feel like

Approving the benchmark with a limit you chose, saying no to anything you do
not understand, and closing the laptop sure that nothing can run up the card.
